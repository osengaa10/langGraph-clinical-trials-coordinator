**Medical History:**

The patient was diagnosed with breast cancer in August 2016, after presenting with a palpable right breast mass. The diagnostic workup revealed a 3.5 cm right breast mass, suspicious for malignancy, and two suspicious axillary lymph nodes. A breast MRI confirmed the presence of a 3.8 cm right breast mass and three pathological appearing lymph nodes. An ultrasound-guided biopsy of the right breast revealed an invasive ductal carcinoma, Nottingham grade 3, estrogen receptor (ER) positive, progesterone receptor (PR) positive, and human epidermal growth receptor 2 (HER2) positive.

**Treatment History:**

The patient underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy with paclitaxel, trastuzumab, and pertuzumab, followed by dose-dense doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide with neulasta support. She then underwent a bilateral mastectomy with right axillary sentinel lymph node biopsy, which revealed residual disease. The patient initiated adjuvant trastuzumab and tamoxifen, but developed severe headaches and nausea, leading to the discovery of brain metastases. She underwent gamma knife surgery and subsequently changed to ado-trastuzumab emtansine. Due to liver metastasis, the patient's treatment was changed to capecitabine/lapatinib, but she experienced progressive disease and was started on experimental pembrolizumab as part of a clinical trial.

**Current Status:**

The patient has achieved a complete radiographic response to pembrolizumab after three months of treatment. She has a history of metastatic disease to the brain and liver, and her molecular features include ER+, PR+, HER2+, and PD-L1+.
